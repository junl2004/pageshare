Top right of the cell, the eye shows how each term of z links to its weight, and the cell icon overlays a real neuron. Blue is the input, red is the cell, green is the output. A thicker arrow means a larger weight.

Dendrites (blue) bring the inputs x in, and a thicker branch means a bigger weight, a stronger synapse. The cell body (red) is the Σ that adds everything up. The axon (green) carries the output y out, and its sheaths light up when the cell fires.

Claude's note · why does the AI look much smarter than the cat?

  • Not because it has more weights. The cat and the model both have trillions of weights (about 1 and 5 trillion here). What differs is the job each was trained for.
  • It was trained for one thing: predicting text, using a huge amount of compute. Evolution trained the cat to survive and hunt.
  • It learned from human writing, trillions of words that billions of human brains produced. It carries their knowledge, which a cat never could.
  • It is tested on its home ground: language, code and exam questions. On those it wins.
  • The cat still wins at moving through the real world, learning from a few tries, learning for life and handling surprises.

So "smart" mostly measures how well the training matches the test, not how many weights there are.
